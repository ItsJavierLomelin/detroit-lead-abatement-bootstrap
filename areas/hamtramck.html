Lead Paint Abatement in Hamtramck, MI

Hamtramck is a small city surrounded by Detroit, filled with early twentieth-century housing built for factory workers and the immigrants who staffed them. Nearly all of it predates the 1978 lead paint ban, so peeling porches and rubbing window sashes are everyday lead questions here.

A city inside Detroit, built by industry

The Detroit Historical Society counts Hamtramck among the communities whose growth followed industry, immigration and the automobile economy. Dense blocks of worker housing went up in the 1910s and 1920s, and much of that stock is still standing.

Houses that old have been painted many times, often over original lead-based coatings. Decades of turnover mean many Hamtramck homes carry layers of repair history and no documentation of what is under the paint.

What that means for lead

A rental turnover is a useful moment to separate a general property inspection from a lead hazard investigation. If tenants report recurring dust near painted windows, document the room and component instead of assuming a fresh coat of paint answers the complaint.

For a multi-unit Hamtramck property, identify the affected unit, shared stairs and common entry areas in the request. A scope limited to one room should not silently become a claim about the whole building. Ask the local property authority about its rental requirements; Detroit’s BSEED process is not Hamtramck’s process.

Common questions

My Hamtramck house was built around 1920. Is lead paint likely?

Very likely. EPA reports that 87 percent of homes built before 1940 have some lead-based paint, and most of Hamtramck's housing comes from that era. Testing shows which surfaces actually carry it.

Tenants keep finding dust near the windows. What should I ask for?

Describe the rooms and components where the dust returns instead of ordering a repaint. A risk assessment can test the friction surfaces and dust, and the report shows whether cleanup, window work or monitoring fits.
