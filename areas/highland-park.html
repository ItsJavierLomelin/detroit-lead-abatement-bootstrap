Lead Paint Abatement in Highland Park, MI

Highland Park grew around the Ford Model T plant, and its streets mix early mansions with dense worker housing from the 1910s and 1920s. That age makes lead paint a fair assumption until testing says otherwise, especially on porches, trim and original windows.

A factory town with grand old housing

The Detroit Historical Society includes Highland Park alongside Detroit and Hamtramck in its account of industrial growth and the automobile industry. The housing boom came early and fast, and much of it predates the 1978 ban by half a century or more.

Many Highland Park houses have seen long vacancies and patchwork repairs. Flaking porch paint and deteriorated exterior trim are common, and every scrape or sanding job on those surfaces can spread contaminated dust.

What that means for lead

A porch repair can cross from cosmetic work into paint disturbance before anyone thinks about lead. When coatings on railings or trim are flaking, request evaluation before scraping. A photograph can show deterioration, but it cannot establish whether lead is present.

Keep a Highland Park permit or property notice with the project file rather than substituting Detroit rental instructions. If exterior work is planned, describe accessible ground below the painted surface and whether children use that space. Those details help an assessor decide whether soil belongs in the investigation.

Common questions

The porch paint is peeling badly. Can I just scrape and repaint it?

EPA advises against removing lead-based paint yourself, and dry scraping is the fastest way to spread contaminated dust. Have the paint tested first; if it is lead, trained and certified people should handle the work.

Is bare soil near the house a lead concern?

It can be, where years of exterior paint have flaked near the foundation. A risk assessor can decide whether soil sampling makes sense for the spots children or pets use.
