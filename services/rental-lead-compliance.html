Rental Lead Documentation for Detroit Property Owners

If a notice, inspector or housing program asks for lead documentation, start with the exact requirement in writing. Detroit’s rental process has changed, and an older online checklist may describe steps that no longer apply to every property.

Check the current Detroit process

BSEED’s current rental certificate page describes a new system based on a 15-point property-condition inspection. An older City page still describes a separate lead-report step. Confirm the applicable requirements with BSEED for your property rather than assuming the older process is universal.

The work may have separate obligations

A simplified rental certification process does not turn a lead hazard into safe paint or erase requirements tied to paid renovation, abatement, a health order or a federally assisted housing program. Keep the reason for the lead work separate from the general property inspection.

Bring the actual notice

Have the property address, unit count, building age, deadline and agency correspondence ready. Ask which report is needed, who may prepare it and how it is submitted. A private provider cannot issue the City’s Certificate of Compliance or guarantee approval.

Common questions

Does Detroit still require a separate lead report for rentals?

BSEED's current rental certificate page describes a system based on a 15-point property-condition inspection, while older city guidance still lists a separate lead-report step. Confirm with BSEED which requirement applies to your property.

Can a contractor issue my Certificate of Compliance?

No. Only the City issues it. A private provider cannot guarantee approval, so treat any such promise as a warning sign.

Does the rental process cover lead-safe renovation rules?

No. Paid renovation, abatement, health orders and housing-program obligations each carry their own requirements, separate from the general property inspection.
