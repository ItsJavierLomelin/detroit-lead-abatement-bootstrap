Corktown: planning a lead-safe renovation before the first scrape

Corktown’s older homes are part of what makes the neighborhood recognizable. When a project involves porch trim, windows or painted interiors, keeping that character should start with knowing what materials the work will disturb.

Why the local context matters

The Detroit Historical Society documents Corktown’s Federal-style houses and later Victorian buildings, along with the neighborhood’s historic-district status. That history supports checking building records and painted components early. It does not prove that any particular house contains lead. A test, not a neighborhood name, answers that question.

Write the project before choosing the method

List each component you plan to change: sash, frame, porch rail, exterior trim or room finish. Keep the inspection report with that list. If the concern is dust from a rubbing window, ask whether a risk assessment and component-specific control are more useful than a whole-house stripping proposal. Different surfaces may need different solutions.

Preservation approval is a separate step

For a property inside a local historic district, discuss exterior work with Detroit’s Historic District Commission before ordering or removing components. A lead professional can develop a hazard-control plan, but that plan does not grant preservation approval. Bring both sets of requirements into the written scope so they do not conflict halfway through the job.

Do not create the hazard while investigating it

Avoid sanding suspect paint to “see what is underneath.” EPA’s renovation guidance explains why paid disturbance of paint in applicable older housing requires lead-safe practices. Ask the firm about qualifications, containment, occupant protection and cleanup. If the purpose is permanent hazard elimination, verify abatement qualifications rather than relying on an RRP certificate alone.

A useful first request

Send the building age if known, the project list, any earlier lead report and photos of deteriorated surfaces. Photos describe a condition; they are not a lead test. Ask what evaluation is needed before work and what final documentation will show the project is complete. No neighborhood-wide promise can replace a property-specific scope.

Common questions

Do I need Historic District Commission approval for a Corktown renovation?

For a property inside the local historic district, discuss exterior work with the Commission before ordering or removing components. A lead plan does not grant preservation approval.

Can I test the paint before the renovation plan is final?

Yes, and that is the right order. Bring the project list to the inspection so the report identifies the components the work will disturb.
