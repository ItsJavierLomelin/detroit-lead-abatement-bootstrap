Downtown Detroit: lead planning for a business district in transition

A downtown building conversion can change who uses a space and how it is maintained. Lead paint planning belongs in that early discussion, before crews disturb old coatings and before the project assumes that commercial and residential rules are the same.

Adaptive reuse changes the questions

The Downtown Detroit Partnership discusses office-space conversion in the district. Converting an office building to housing is a different planning problem from repainting an office suite. Document the present use, proposed use, building age and the surfaces the work will disturb.

Commercial-only is not automatically RRP target housing

The EPA renovation rule focuses on applicable pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities. Do not automatically apply that residential framework to every downtown storefront or office. At the same time, a commercial classification is not permission to expose workers or occupants to lead dust. Ask the project’s safety and environmental professionals which worker-protection and waste requirements apply.

Separate the investigations

Paint testing, asbestos surveys, structural inspections and other environmental reviews answer different questions. A lead paint report does not establish that all other hazardous materials are absent. For mixed-use or changing-use projects, the scope should say which spaces and components were evaluated and which were not.

Plan access and occupancy

A multi-floor project may have occupied spaces, shared entrances and work occurring in stages. Describe those conditions to the professional preparing the control plan. Ask how occupants, deliveries and later contractors will be kept out of affected areas. Avoid promises about completion or reopening until the findings, approvals and scope are known.

A business-district request that gets a useful answer

Provide the intended use, unit or floor count, construction history, renovation drawings and any existing reports. State whether you need evaluation, permanent hazard control or renovation safety planning. For housing approvals, verify current Detroit requirements directly with BSEED. A lead contractor cannot guarantee a Certificate of Compliance or decide whether a proposed conversion is permitted.

Common questions

Do residential lead rules apply to an office conversion?

The EPA renovation rule focuses on applicable pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities. A conversion to housing changes that analysis, so document the present and proposed use and ask which framework applies.

What should a conversion lead request include?

The intended use, unit or floor count, construction history, renovation drawings and any existing reports, plus whether you need evaluation, permanent hazard control or renovation safety planning.
